## Context: the page “Particle model”

Substances consist of tiny particles. This is how you explain solid, liquid and gaseous.

### Why

Why does water flow, but a stone does not? Why does air fill a whole room? We cannot see the smallest parts of substances. With a model we imagine them and use it to explain what we observe.

### Rule

- **Particle model**: In the particle model every substance consists of very small particles, which you imagine as balls.

The three states of matter in the particle model:
- State | Arrangement of the particles | Movement
- solid | close together, in order | vibrate in place
- liquid | close together, not in order | change places
- gaseous | far apart | move freely through the space

When a substance gets warmer, its particles move faster. With enough heat they break out of their fixed order: the substance melts. With even more heat they fly apart: the substance boils.

### Example

**Example: Water is heated**

Ice is solid. The water particles sit close together. At 0 °C it melts, then they are mobile and the water flows. At 100 °C it boils: the particles fly apart and it is water vapour.

Interactive: A box with 30 particles and a slider for the temperature. At a low temperature (solid) the particles sit close together and vibrate in place. At a medium temperature (liquid) they are close together and change places. At a high temperature (gaseous) they are far apart and move freely through the whole box.

### Exercise zustand-beschreibung

- Task: Match each state with the fitting description in the particle model.
- Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
- Solution: solid – particles close together, vibrate in place; liquid – particles close together, change places; gaseous – particles far apart, free to move
- Explanation: The higher the temperature, the more mobile the particles. In the solid state they have fixed places, in the liquid state they change places, in the gaseous state they fly freely.

### Exercise wasser-fliesst

- Task: Why does water flow?
- Answer format: choose one answer
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ The particles are close together but change places. – In a liquid the particles touch but can slide past each other. That is why it flows.
  - ✗ The particles sit fixed in one place. – Particles have fixed places in a solid, for example in ice. That is why ice keeps its shape.
  - ✗ The particles are far apart. – Particles are far apart in a gas, for example in water vapour.
- Solution: The particles are close together but change places.
- Explanation: In a liquid the particles change places. That is why it takes the shape of its container and flows.

### Exercise teilchen-groesser

- Task: When a substance is heated, its particles get bigger.
- Answer format: choose “true” or “false”
- Solution: The statement is false.
- Explanation: The particles stay the same size. They only move faster and therefore often need more room.
