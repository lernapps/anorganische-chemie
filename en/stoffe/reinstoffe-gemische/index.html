Why

Almost everything around you is a substance: water, air, metal, milk. To sort substances, first ask: is this a single kind of substance, or are several mixed together?

Rule

Pure substance

A pure substance consists of only one kind of substance, for example pure water or pure oxygen.

Mixture

A mixture consists of several substances, for example air, milk or granite.

A pure substance always has the same properties, for example a fixed boiling point. The substances in a mixture can often be separated again without a chemical reaction, for example by filtering or evaporating.

Example

Is air a pure substance?

Air consists mainly of nitrogen and oxygen, plus other gases. So several substances are mixed. Air is therefore a mixture, not a pure substance.

Practice

Exercise

Which of these substances are mixtures?

  • air right answer

    Air consists of nitrogen, oxygen and other gases. Several substances are mixed.

  • milk right answer

    Milk contains water, fats and proteins, among other things. It is a mixture.

  • pure water

    Pure (distilled) water consists only of water. It is a pure substance.

  • pure oxygen

    Pure oxygen consists of only one kind of substance. It is a pure substance.

Mixtures consist of several substances. Air and milk are mixtures; pure water and pure oxygen are pure substances.

Right answer: The statement is false.
