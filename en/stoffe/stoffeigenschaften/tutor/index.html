Context: the page “Properties of substances”

State of matter, melting and boiling point: this is how you recognise substances and infer their use.

Why

Why are power cables made of copper and pots made of metal, but drinks made of liquids? Every substance has properties that help you recognise it and that show what it is good for.

Rule

Three properties help to sort substances:

  • The state of matter at room temperature (20 °C): solid, liquid or gaseous.

  • The melting point: from this temperature a solid becomes liquid.

  • The boiling point: from this temperature a liquid becomes gaseous.

  • Melting point: The temperature at which a solid becomes liquid. For a pure substance it is always the same.

  • Boiling point: The temperature at which a liquid becomes gaseous. For a pure substance it is always the same.

Remember: Memory aid

If room temperature (20 °C) is below the melting point, the substance is solid. If it lies between the melting and the boiling point, it is liquid. If it is above, it is gaseous.

Example

Example: Why is water liquid at 20 °C?

Water melts at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C. Room temperature (20 °C) lies in between. That is why water is liquid at 20 °C.

Exercise kupfer-kabel

  • Task: Why are power cables made of copper?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Copper conducts electric current well. – Copper is a metal that conducts current very well. That is why it is used in wires and cables.
    • ✗ Copper is liquid at room temperature. – Copper is solid at 20 °C. It only melts at about 1085 °C.
    • ✗ Copper has a very low boiling point. – Copper only boils at about 2595 °C. A low boiling point would be no advantage for a cable anyway.
  • Solution: Copper conducts electric current well.
  • Explanation: You choose a substance for the property you need. A cable has to conduct current well, and copper does.
