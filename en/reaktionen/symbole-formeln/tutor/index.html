Context: the page “Symbols and formulas”

Every element has a symbol, every compound a formula. This is how you read and write them.

Why

Chemists do not write “water consists of hydrogen and oxygen”, they write H₂O. Symbols and formulas are a shorthand that is read the same way all over the world.

Rule

  • Compound: A compound consists of atoms of different elements joined in a fixed ratio, for example water or carbon dioxide.

  • Molecule: A molecule is a small particle made of several joined atoms, for example a water molecule.

  • Formula: A formula uses element symbols to show which atoms a substance is made of. A small subscript number, the index, tells how many atoms there are.

The formula of water is $\ce{H2O}$: one molecule has 2 hydrogen atoms (H) and 1 oxygen atom (O). If there is no index, it is 1 atom.

Some formulas and their atoms:

  • Substance | Formula | Atoms in one particle
  • water | $\ce{H2O}$ | 2 H, 1 O
  • carbon dioxide | $\ce{CO2}$ | 1 C, 2 O
  • methane | $\ce{CH4}$ | 1 C, 4 H
  • ammonia | $\ce{NH3}$ | 1 N, 3 H
  • oxygen | $\ce{O2}$ | 2 O

Example

Example: Reading the formula of methane

Methane has the formula $\ce{CH4}$. C stands for carbon, H for hydrogen. The 4 says: for 1 carbon atom there are 4 hydrogen atoms. A methane molecule has 5 atoms in total.

Exercise atome-ammoniak

  • Task: How many hydrogen atoms does one particle of ammonia with the formula NH₃ have?
  • Answer format: type a number
  • Solution: 3
  • Explanation: The index 3 is after the H. It applies only to the H, so there are 3 hydrogen atoms and 1 nitrogen atom.
  • Typical mistakes and what the app says:
    • 1: The N stands for nitrogen. The index 3 after the H says there are 3 hydrogen atoms.
    • 4: You counted all atoms. The question asks only for the hydrogen atoms.
